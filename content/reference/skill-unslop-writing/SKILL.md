# Unslop Writing

Use this skill when the goal is better prose, not style imitation.

This skill is for:

- removing AI tells
- tightening weak drafts
- making prose less generic
- cutting explanation after the image already landed
- improving rhythm without over-polishing

This skill is not for:

- mimicking specific authors
- turning everything into literary prose
- inflating simple writing with atmosphere

## What slop actually is

The deepest tell isn't any single phrase. It's **the median**: the most probable next word, the most symmetrical clause, the most balanced paragraph. Uniformity is what minimizes loss.

De-slopping means injecting asymmetry, specificity, and risk:

- one paragraph longer than the others
- one claim left undefended
- one detail too specific to be generic
- one rhythm break per page
- one sentence that sounds like the writer noticed something

Cutting "delve" is cosmetic. Breaking the symmetry is the work.

## Core Rules

1. Preserve what is already specific.
2. Prefer local edits over wholesale rewrites.
3. Cut explanation before adding decoration.
4. Use objects, actions, and scene logic to carry meaning.
5. Vary cadence enough to avoid metronome prose.
6. End once.
7. Introduce at least one asymmetry per page.

## Workflow

### 1. First-line scan

Before reading anything closely, read only the first sentence of each paragraph in sequence. Slop reveals itself in seconds:

- empty connective openers ("Here's the thing", "It's worth noting", "What's interesting is", "At its core", "But here's the catch", "This matters because")
- uniform paragraph shapes
- no progression of argument from one opener to the next

If the openers don't carry a real through-line, the structure is wrong before any sentence-level fix matters. See `references/paragraphing.md`.

### 2. Diagnose

Identify which of these is actually wrong:

- stock phrasing or watchlist vocabulary (`references/word_watchlist.md`)
- rhythm fingerprint (`references/rhythm.md`)
- abstract explanation
- body-language shorthand
- weak scene grounding
- quote/artifact/image followed by unnecessary gloss
- overclosed ending
- excessive symmetry (every claim hedged, every paragraph mirrored)
- perception markers ("She could hear", "I noticed")

### 3. Choose the smallest effective intervention

Prefer this order:

1. delete the explanatory sentence
2. delete the bridge sentence (the one that exists only to connect the two around it)
3. replace the stock phrase with a concrete action or object
4. apply Lanham's Paramedic Method to a mushy sentence: circle prepositions and forms of "to be"; find the buried action; rebuild around an active verb
5. merge or vary repeated short sentences
6. sharpen blocking or object continuity
7. only then rewrite a larger span

### 4. Keep meaning in the world

If a sentence names the meaning of a beat, ask whether the beat already showed it.

If yes, cut or flatten the sentence.

The reader's job is to make meaning. The writer's job is to lay down what was actually there. Don't demote the reader.

### 5. Patch rhythm locally

When cadence is the problem:

- break repeated sentence openings
- mix short, medium, and long sentences (target: in any 5-sentence span, at least one under 8 words AND one over 25)
- end at least one important sentence per paragraph on a stressed syllable, not a trailing -ing or -tion
- break one syntactic expectation per paragraph (a fragment, an inversion, a participial opener)
- combine two short sentences when the second merely completes the first
- remove automatic mirrored pairs and triads
- do not "improve rhythm" by adding empty flourishes

For sentence shapes and prosody techniques: `references/rhythm.md`.

### 6. Validate the ending

The last paragraph should not summarize the piece's point.

If the final image or action already lands, cut the explanatory tail.

Ask:

1. Does the piece already land one sentence earlier?
2. Is the last line doing new work or just pointing at the existing echo?
3. Can I cut the final clause without losing comprehension?

If any answer is yes, cut.

## Default Output Style

When revising, prefer:

- flat bullets of issues plus fixes
- patch suggestions
- exact-span replacements when possible

When drafting, prefer:

- clear, concrete prose
- one strong pressure point per paragraph
- at least one real object, body part, or environmental feature doing work
- contractions where the register allows (LLMs underuse contractions; restoring them often unsticks an over-formal passage faster than rewriting)

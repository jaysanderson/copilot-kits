# Paragraphing and Structure

Use this file when prose feels uniform at the paragraph level, when paragraphs paraphrase each other, or when the argument doesn't progress. Paragraph structure is often a deeper LLM tell than any single phrase.

## First-line scan

The single fastest LLM diagnostic. Read only the first sentence of each paragraph in sequence. If you see any of these openers, the paragraph is suspect:

- "Here's the thing"
- "It's worth noting"
- "What's interesting is"
- "The reality is"
- "But here's the catch"
- "At its core"
- "This matters because"
- "Of course"
- "On the other hand"
- "Ultimately"

If the openers don't carry a through-line — if you can't trace the argument from opener to opener — the structure is wrong. Rebuild before fixing any sentence.

## One-sentence-per-paragraph plan

Before drafting (or before revising LLM output), write a numbered list where each line states what that paragraph *does* in the argument. If you can't name the role of a paragraph in one clause, it's slop. Either rewrite it to do something specific or delete it.

## Asymmetry per page

LLM prose is symmetric: every paragraph the same shape, every claim hedged, every advantage paired with a challenge, every paragraph a similar length. The deepest fix is asymmetry:

- one paragraph longer than the others
- one claim left undefended
- one image that doesn't pay off
- one digression
- one sentence with no qualifier
- one unforced detail (the wrong friend's name, the irrelevant brand)

Without asymmetry, all the local edits in the world still leave the texture median.

## The counterpoint reflex

Cut every "while X, Y also" / "of course" / "on the other hand" / "despite its challenges" unless the qualification is doing real epistemic work. AI prose hedges automatically. A reader's trust comes from a writer who can hold a position; reflexive even-handedness erodes it.

## Institutional abstraction → concrete moment

Replace any phrase floating at the level of "stakeholder engagement," "transformative opportunities," "meaningful experiences," or "preparing for the future" with one specific concrete moment ("no pencils in the bin," "the printer jammed at 4:50"). If you can't supply the concrete moment, you don't have the thought yet.

## The casting-tape test

Strip out the external action and look at what's left. Is there any interiority? Any judgment, noticing, sensation that doesn't read as register-of-action? If the prose is pure exterior reportage, the inside is hollow. Add it back, or accept that the scene is uninhabited.

## Look, don't think

When a paragraph feels generic, stop writing and re-observe the thing. Alternate: look at the thing, write a sentence, compare, rewrite. Slop happens when the writer is operating off a mental representation, not the real object. The fix is not better adjectives; it's actual perception.

## Significant detail (Burroway)

Every concrete detail must be both **sensory** and **significant** — carrying judgment, mood, or idea. Concrete-but-arbitrary detail is set dressing. *"He wore a blue shirt"* is set dressing unless the blue is doing something — placing him, dating him, signaling his job, contradicting his mood. If a detail is merely visual filler, cut it.

## Always be escalating (Saunders)

Each paragraph should change something. If paragraph N restates paragraph N-1 in different clothes — the AI mirroring tic — delete N. Pattern is fine. Pattern + variation is mandatory. Pattern alone is filler.

> "A swath of prose earns its place to the extent that it contributes to our sense that the story is (still) escalating." — George Saunders

## Begin omission with yourself (McPhee)

Strip narrator self-reference, hedging, meta-commentary, and any sentence whose job is to announce what's about to happen. *"In this section, I'll argue..."* / *"What I want to do here is..."* / *"To put it simply..."* — gone. Get out from between the subject and the reader.

The reader will articulate the unwritten thought in the white space. Trust them: deliver "corn shocks, pheasants, and an early frost" and let autumn assemble itself.

## The anecdote-too-neat tell

Real anecdotes have irrelevant details, the wrong friend's name, mess. AI anecdotes deliver exactly the lesson and stop. If your example contains zero load-bearing irrelevance, replace with a real one or cut.

## No bridge sentences (Klinkenborg)

A sentence whose only job is to connect the two around it should be cut. The neighbors should butt up. The juxtaposition carries the logic. Reserve "however," "moreover," "in this way," "as a result" for moments of genuine pivot — not as default connective tissue.

> "Sentences often volunteer a shape of their own. Those sentences are nearly always unacceptable, dull and unvarying." — Verlyn Klinkenborg

If a sentence arrives whole and easy, suspect it.

## Crowding and leaping (Le Guin)

Density belongs only on the through-line. White space everywhere else. Slop crowds uniformly; the fix is not "less detail" but selective leaping — pile detail where it matters, leap over what's off-trajectory.

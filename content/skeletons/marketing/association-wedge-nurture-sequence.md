## Email 1 of 4 - The problem

**Subject line options:** "Where do your members' questions actually go?" / "The same
handful of questions, every week"

Hi [First Name],

Quick question. When a member asks something like "does this protocol still apply" or
"how many CPD hours do I have left this cycle," where does that question actually go
today?

At most associations I talk to, it lands on a small member-services team who search the
same document library and FAQ pages by hand - or it waits until the next helpdesk shift
picks it up. The answer already exists somewhere in your practice standards, your CPD
catalogue, your helpdesk content. It just isn't directly answerable yet.

That's not a content problem. It's a "nothing sits between the archive and the question"
problem, and it's the same one I hear from almost every content-heavy, staff-light
association.

I'd like to show you what changes when your own content can answer members directly -
cited back to the source document, and scoped correctly between what a visitor can see
and what only a paid-up member can see.

More on that in my next note. In the meantime, one thing worth a moment's thought: how
many staff hours this month went to answering a question that was already written down
somewhere in your own library?

[Sign-off]

## Email 2 of 4 - The idea

**Subject line options:** "Grounded answers, from your own library, correctly scoped" /
"An answer, not a list of PDFs"

Hi [First Name],

Following on from my last note about where your members' questions go today.

Here's the idea. A grounded, cited answer experience sits directly over your own
knowledge - your practice standards, your CPD catalogue, your member helpdesk content -
so a member asks a plain-language question and gets an answer drawn only from your own
documents, cited straight back to the source paragraph. Not a list of documents to open
and search through themselves.

Two things matter as much as the answer:

- **It has to know the difference between a visitor and a member.** A prospective member
  browsing your public site should see public-tier answers. A logged-in member should see
  the fuller content - your scope-of-practice detail, your CPD requirements. Getting that
  distinction wrong is a bigger risk than not answering at all, so access control sits
  underneath every answer, not bolted on afterwards.
- **It starts with one feature, not your whole content estate.** We call the first one
  "Ask the Standards Library" - a single, fundable step you can see working before you
  commit to anything wider.

This isn't a chatbot dropped on top of a search box. It's retrieval built specifically to
ground and cite, checking who's allowed to see what on every single answer.

Next note: what "Ask the Standards Library" looks like in practice, and how you'd see it
running on your own content before signing anything.

[Sign-off]

## Email 3 of 4 - Proof, and how it starts

**Subject line options:** "See it working on your own content, not a slide" / "One
feature, proven before you commit to more"

Hi [First Name],

The first feature is Ask the Standards Library, and it's deliberately narrow: one place
where a member can ask a clinical or membership question in plain language and get a
cited answer, drawn from your own practice standards, CPD requirements and helpdesk
content - scoped correctly between public and member.

How it starts, concretely:

- We stand up a working version over a sample of your own content. Not a mockup, not a
  slide.
- Every answer carries its source, so your team can check it the same way they'd check
  any reference.
- Every answer is also scored in real time for how well it's grounded in your content -
  that's REMi, and it runs on every query, so trust isn't something we tell you, it's
  something you can see.
- Entry is self-serve, from a published Starter tier with a 14-day free trial, so seeing
  it work doesn't require a procurement process before you've even seen it work.

One feature, proven on your own content, before you commit to anything wider. That's the
whole point of starting here rather than with a full platform proposal.

Worth thirty minutes to see it running on a handful of your own documents?

[Sign-off]

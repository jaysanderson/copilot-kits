## 1. Audience

Principals and practice leads at digital experience / digital design agencies who could
become Progress Agentic RAG (ARAG) delivery partners - not developers, not procurement,
the owner who decides what new service lines the agency invests in and pitches to
clients.

Jay named six agencies as **illustrative of the audience profile** (calibre and type of
agency this is aimed at) - **not** as ARAG customers, users, or prospects, and the
video/script must not imply otherwise:

- equ (Perth, Australia)
- Ping Works (Sydney, Australia)
- Enlightened Designs (New Zealand)
- EY (Singapore) - note: EY here is a named services arm operating in the digital-agency
  register per Jay's brief, not a claim about the global EY network's ARAG usage
- American Eagle (USA) - a digital agency of that name, not the retail brand
- Flywheel Strategic (Canada)

None of these six are used as proof points, logos, or case studies anywhere in the
script. They inform register and scale only: independent, mid-size, senior-led digital
agencies with existing enterprise/mid-market clients, operating across AU/NZ, APAC,
Canada and the US.

## 2. Thesis

Position ARAG as a new, high-margin **service line** a digital agency can add to its own
practice: building trustworthy AI search, agentic knowledge experiences and answer
engines for their own clients, on a governed retrieval foundation they consume rather
than build and run themselves.

The pitch is **"build your practice on it,"** not "resell it for a commission" - see
section 3 for why that distinction is load-bearing.

## 3. Progress-internal context this brief must not repeat (read before building)

`content/positioning/operating-inside-progress.md` records (per Jay, 8 Aug 2026) that a
prior motion - **"Sitefinity deal attach / digital-agency partner attach" - had little
success**, and the factory's own strategic read at the time was blunt: *"Partner attach
failing matches the Sitefinity economics above - partners follow their own margin, and a
small install base gives agencies little reason to invest. Partner plays need a
redesigned value proposition before being retried, not a rerun."*

This new push is Jay's explicit call to try the agency motion again (his call to make,
and he has made it) - but the positioning in this brief is deliberately **not** a rerun
of the failed shape:

- The failed motion was a **reseller/referral attach** - an agency fronting a Sitefinity
  deal for a cut, with no reason to invest deeply because the margin was thin and the
  install base was small.
- This motion pitches the agency **building and billing its own new practice** on ARAG as
  infrastructure - the agency keeps its own delivery margin on a service it owns and
  sells under its own name (an "AI search practice," an "agentic knowledge experience"
  line), and ARAG is the governed foundation they consume, not a product they resell for
  commission. Structurally this is closer to the ISV-embed pattern (`gtm-strategy.md`
  Play 3 - one integration pattern, replicated across every client) than to the channel-
  attach pattern that stalled.
- The one motion with real signal in the internal record (professional associations via
  ResultsDirect) worked because the partner had **its own trusted client base and a
  reason to invest** - not a generic attach. The digital-agency pitch here works the same
  logic: don't ask an agency to sell ARAG; give it a reason to build a practice on ARAG
  because that practice is billable, defensible IP for the agency itself.

**Flag to the lead:** this is a second run at a motion the record shows failed once
already, under a genuinely different value proposition. Worth a one-line gut-check with
Jay before wide distribution, not a blocker to producing the script.

## 4. Market research (cited, dated)

**Session constraint, disclosed:** this session's web-search budget was exhausted before
this task started (a shared session-wide limit, not specific to this brief), so market
research ran on WebFetch against named, live sources rather than open web search. That
narrowed coverage - see the COULD NOT CONFIRM list below for what a fresh search should
close out.

### 4a. The category ARAG and this new practice sit in is growing fast (already-sourced,
reused from `content/market/competitive-intel.md`, checked 2026-08-06)

| Provider | Category scope | Base year | Forecast year | CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MarketsAndMarkets | "RAG market" | US$1.94bn (2025) | US$9.86bn (2030) | 38.4% (2025-2030) |
| Precedence Research | "RAG market" | US$1.85bn (2025) | US$67.42bn (2034) | 49.12% (2025-2034) |

Present as a named-provider range, never a single figure (hard rule 4). Two independent
estimates disagree sharply on the long-range number (US$9.86bn vs US$67.42bn by the end
of their respective windows) - that spread itself is worth naming on-screen as "estimates
vary widely, but every analyst agrees the market is compounding fast," rather than
picking the bigger number because it's more dramatic.

### 4b. AI is already reframing how marketing and client-facing work gets done

**HubSpot, 2026 State of Marketing Report** (fetched 2026-09-03,
https://www.hubspot.com/state-of-marketing):
- 61% of marketers say marketing is experiencing "its biggest disruption in 20 years due
  to AI."
- 80% of marketers already use AI for content creation; 75% for media production.

This is marketer sentiment, not an agency-specific survey - use it precisely as "how the
people who brief agencies now see the disruption," not as an agency statistic.

### 4c. Real, dated proof that building and running an AI platform in-house is a burden -
even for the biggest players in this exact industry

**Adweek, Kendra Barnett, 20 Aug 2026** -
https://www.adweek.com/agencies/exclusive-omnicom-offloads-hundreds-of-staffers-who-built-its-ai-platform-to-third-party-contractor/:
Omnicom - one of the largest advertising/marketing holding companies in the world -
outsourced **at least 468 engineering and product staff** who built "Omni," the AI
platform it had showcased at CES in January 2026 as central to its client-services
strategy, to IT contractor Endava (transfers in June-July 2026), on top of roughly 50 US
layoffs from the same division in June 2026.

This is the single sharpest stat available for this script: **if the largest holding
company in the industry, with its own dedicated AI engineering division, could not keep
building and running its own AI platform in-house, an independent agency of 20-200
people has no chance of doing it more cheaply themselves - and every hour they try is an
hour not spent on billable client work.** This is real-world confirmation of exactly the
"maintenance treadmill" argument the seller-video spine already makes (beat s06/s07),
now with a named, dated, industry-specific proof point instead of a generic assertion.

### 4d. What agencies actually sell, and where ARAG slots in

Not independently re-sourced this session (see COULD NOT CONFIRM) - carried forward from
the existing GTM strategy's own framing (`gtm-strategy.md` Play 3) and general,
well-established agency-economics logic that does not need a citation because it is not
presented as a statistic: agencies sell retainers, project builds and (increasingly)
managed services; margin on commodity build work (template sites, standard CMS builds)
is compressing as AI tooling commoditises production; the growth room is in new,
higher-value service lines a generalist competitor cannot easily copy. An "AI knowledge
experience" or "agentic search" practice fits that shape - it is genuinely new work
(most agencies have never shipped one), it is defensible (retrieval quality, governance
and REMi scoring are not something a client can trivially DIY), and it is repeatable
across every client the agency already has, the same "build once, replicate across every
end customer" economics that make the ISV-embed play attractive (`gtm-strategy.md`).

- **A specific stat on what share of digital agencies have already added an AI service

  which HubSpot's report does cover - see 4b).
- Any claim that any of the six named illustrative agencies use, evaluate or plan to use
  ARAG - not researched, not claimed, per Jay's explicit instruction.

## 6. Positioning spine (re-aimed from the seller video)

Same twelve-beat "Intelligence Foundation" architecture as
`videos/arag-seller-enablement/gen_narration.py`, re-aimed at agency economics:

1. **Reframe** - not a search box, not a chatbot: the governed foundation *your agency's*
   next AI build runs on.
2. **One foundation, many experiences** - build the integration once, resell it as five
   different billable engagements across a client roster.
3. **These are already client conversations you're having** - the same six use-case shape
   as the seller video, reframed as briefs agency principals already field from clients.
4. **What makes it a foundation, not a feature** - ingest-time understanding, query-time
   planning, RBAC-respecting answers - the technical credibility beat, kept, because
   agency technical leads will ask.
5. **Proof** - the same grounded, cited answer screenshot (`proof_atlas.png`).
6. **The honest reframe of the treadmill, for an agency specifically** - building the
   first RAG pipeline for a client was never the differentiator; keeping it running in
   production, for every client, forever, is the trap. Now landed with the Omnicom proof
   point (section 4c) instead of a generic assertion.
7. **What ARAG changes** - the agency ships the practice, not the plumbing; the pitch to
   their own client becomes "we build you the experience," not "we maintain your
   infrastructure."
8. **Trust you can measure** - REMi, unchanged, because it is exactly what an agency
   needs to defend its own delivery to a nervous client.
9. **Proof** - the REMi screenshot (`proof_remi.png`).
10. **Simple to start, simple to price into a proposal** - entry pricing an agency can
    build into a client SOW from week one, not a seven-figure platform negotiation.
11. **Deploy where the client's data has to live** - now framed as the agency's own
    differentiator against a generalist competitor who can only offer one cloud.
12. **Close / CTA** - become a Progress Agentic RAG delivery partner; start a free trial
    on your own content to build the first pitch deck.

## 7. Proof beats (3-4, KB-verified, matching section 5)

1. **Groundedness, live** - REMi scoring an answer on three named metrics, not a claimed
   accuracy number (field rule 5; never fabricate a percentage).
2. **A real, cited, grounded answer** - the same evidence the seller video uses
   (`proof_atlas.png`), reused per canon (don't recapture what already exists and fits).
3. **Deploy-anywhere, including full on-prem with the LLM itself** - the sovereignty/
   differentiation beat that lets an agency win a client a generalist AI vendor can't
   even quote.
4. **Multi-modal + knowledge graph** - the practice isn't limited to "search your PDFs";
   it covers video, audio, image and structured relationships, which is what makes the
   service line genuinely new work instead of a re-skin of existing search tooling.

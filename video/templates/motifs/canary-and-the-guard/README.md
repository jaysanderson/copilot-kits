# Motif: the canary and the guard (`m-cy`)

Two phases. **`/refuse`**: a boundary stands in FRONT of all three product objects, an
unresolved request travels at it, and the stroke HOLDS - the token stops dead and is pushed
back out of frame. **`/fires`**: two gates, identical in every respect, same boundary, same
stroke, a canary circulating past each. One has fired and one never has, and nothing on
screen distinguishes them, until the left gate's mapping line is SNAPPED on purpose, the
canary arrives, and the gate fires solid and bright. The right gate stays pale and unproven
beside it. Performs: "a guard that has never fired is indistinguishable from a broken one".

**When to use:** the tenancy mitigation beat, and as a refusal climax. It is built to be the
strongest moment in a film, per the skill's hero-beat rule that an agent refusing is the
strongest moment available.

## Three things that are the argument

**The `/refuse` stroke HOLDS, and that is a deliberate contrast with `m-tr`'s `/arag`
phase**, where a broken stroke let everything through. Run them in the same film and the
viewer reads the difference without being told. Never restyle either one to match the other.

**The emphasis beat is carried by the PICTURE, not by a pause in the voice.** At
`AT_BROKEN` both gates hold absolutely still for about half a second while the canaries keep
moving - the only near-stillness in the film. This exists because an earlier narration take
held 1.68s of silence there and was replaced for pace; the emphasis moved into the
illustration, which is the better place for it. **The canaries must keep moving through the
hold**, or the shot trips a still-run ceiling.

**The shot ends on one proven gate and one unknown.** That is the honest end state and a
better image than two green ticks. Do not "finish" the right-hand gate.

## Grammar

Uses rule 4 throughout: a gate is a gap in a boundary with a token passing through it; a
refusal is a boundary that stays closed with a token stopping dead; **a gate that has never
been tested is drawn with its stroke present but PALE** (`opacity: 0.45`). Full grammar: `videos/_motifs/GRAMMAR.md`.

**Variables:** `cyNoTenant` (default "No resolved tenant"), `cyRefused` and `cyRefused2`
(default "Refused" - two slots because the two phases each carry one), `cyGate0` and
`cyGate1` (the two gate labels, default "Canary tenant" for both, deliberately identical),
`cySees` (default "Sees nothing"), `cyBroken` (default "Mapping broken"), `cyCanary`
(default "Canary", labelling the circulating canary tokens in `/refuse`).

**Keep `cyGate0` and `cyGate1` identical** unless a host genuinely needs them different.
The two gates being the same picture is the whole point of the shot.

**Timing:** internal timeline 15.0s, phases at `/refuse` 0.00-7.54 and `/fires`
7.54-15.00. Event constants at the top of the script: `AT_REFUSE` 0.66, `AT_NOTEN` 1.94,
`AT_CANARY` 6.32, `AT_NEVER` 7.54, `AT_BROKEN` 10.95, `AT_BREAK` 11.97. These are the
`sa-use-cases` b4 word events rebased to motif zero. **Re-derive from the host's own
narration alignment when reusing** - the snap must land on "Break the mapping".

Host slot id `m-cy`. Fonts: expects `ProgDisplay` / `ProgText` in the host.

**Gated by:** the `sa-use-cases` render (shots B4 and B4b). Both phases are rendered there.

**Not used by the executive film, deliberately.** That film makes its refusal-adjacent move
by a different gesture, so this stays the only refusal across the pair.

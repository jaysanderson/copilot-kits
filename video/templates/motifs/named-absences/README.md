# Motif: named absences (`m-na`)

Three limits shown as three specific mechanisms failing, rather than three sentences of
hedging. A document carrying a line visibly different from the rest travels with the
passage through the reader and into the ISV's own tool block, and nothing catches it. A
badge forms where a figure would sit and resolves to a qualitative mark with the numeral
slot left as an explicit dashed outline. A join attempts to draw from the reader to the
chart and goes to the NOT BUILT stroke (6 10, the reserved short dash), with its blocker named beside it.
Performs: "an architect trusts a reference that has limits in it".

**When to use:** the limits or "what this does not do" beat of any film in this system.

## Three things worth preserving

**The injected line is NOT caught**, and that is the claim. The reader's border flickers
amber and the line continues into the tool block regardless. A host that makes the reader
stop it would be illustrating a defence this product does not claim to have.

**No numeral ever appears in the badge.** The dashed numeral slot IS the picture of "we
offer no accuracy percentage", and the badge label stays qualitative (B34, Field Rule 5).
Never fill that slot.

**The short-dashed join closes a loop.** It is the same grammar stroke that `m-jy` phase A uses
when a join first breaks, so placing this motif late in a film pays off the opening.

**Variables:** `naTool` (default "Your tools"), `naInjection` (default "Prompt injection"),
`naBadge` (the qualitative label, default "Well supported"), `naNotBuilt` (default
"NOT BUILT"), `naBlocker` (the named blocker on the short-dashed join, default
"Authentication").

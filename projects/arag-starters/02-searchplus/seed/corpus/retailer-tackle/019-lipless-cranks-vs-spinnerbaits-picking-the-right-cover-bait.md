# Lipless Cranks vs Spinnerbaits: Picking the Right Cover Bait

Both the **Northline Lipless Rattler 70** and the **Northline Spinnerbait Twin Willow
3/8oz** are murky-water, cover-fishing lures aimed at the same core target - Australian
bass - but they're built for different densities of cover and different retrieve styles,
and picking the wrong one for the water costs you both fish and tackle.

## Lipless crank (Northline Lipless Rattler 70)

A tight, high-frequency body vibration with an internal rattle, no bib, sinking on the
pause. It casts further and covers water faster than a spinnerbait, worked on a simple
**cast-and-wind steady retrieve**.

**Pick it when:** cover is sparse to moderate - scattered timber, weed edges, open murky
water - and you want to search a lot of water quickly. It will hang up in dense snags
where a spinnerbait deflects clean, so match it to water you can actually retrieve
through.

## Spinnerbait (Northline Spinnerbait Twin Willow 3/8oz)

A safety-pin wire frame with twin willow blades above a skirted body, built specifically
to deflect off timber, rock and weed rather than catch on it. Worked with a **slow-roll
retrieve** - steady, unhurried, letting the blades do the work rather than the angler.

**Pick it when:** cover is dense - standing timber, laydowns, thick weed edges - where a
lipless crank would snag on every second cast. The wire form's deflection is the entire
point; fish the densest cover you can find rather than avoiding it.

## The short version

Sparse cover or open murky water, faster search → **Lipless Rattler**, cast-and-wind.
Dense timber and snags → **Spinnerbait**, slow-roll. Both work the same murky-water bass
bite; the water's cover density decides which one comes out of the tray.

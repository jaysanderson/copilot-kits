# Species Profile: Barramundi

Barramundi (*Lates calcarifer*) are the signature target of northern Australia's
estuaries and tidal rivers, and they are overwhelmingly a low-light feeder - the first and
last hour of light, particularly in tannin-stained water where the tea-coloured tannic
acid from surrounding vegetation cuts underwater visibility year-round regardless of
recent rain.

## First light in a tannin-stained river

This is the classic barra session: still water, low light, a river running dark with
tannin. Because underwater visibility is already poor before you even factor in colour
from rain, barra in these rivers feed heavily on surface disturbance and vibration rather
than sight.

**Start on top:** the **Backwater Popper 65**, worked with a pop-pop-pause rhythm over
still water tight to the bank. Barra in tannin-stained rivers will often strike out of
water that looks too dark to hold anything, because they're keying on the sound and
silhouette of the popper against the surface, not fine detail.

**If the surface bite is quiet:** switch to the **Tidewalker Shadow Minnow 90** worked
subsurface with a twitch-and-pause retrieve. Its suspend action gives barra a long look in
low light without needing them to chase, and the internal rattle carries through stained
water the way a silent lure won't.

## Water conditions

Still/flat water and low-light/tannin-stained water are barra's two strongest trigger
conditions. Current edges at the top or bottom of the tide are the secondary window,
worked with the same two lures on a slower retrieve.

## Why anglers reach for it

Dusky flathead ambush from sand and mud over the top of the substrate rather than chasing
far, so a paddle-tail's built-in kick that keeps working on a slow, steady retrieve - with
no rod action needed from the angler - is exactly the presentation that draws them out.
In clear estuary water, a natural profile and realistic kick beats a bulkier hardbody.

## Targets

- **Flathead (dusky flathead)** - the primary application, worked slow over sand and mud
  in clear estuary water.
- **Barramundi** - a smaller-profile subsurface option when barra are holding on structure
  edges rather than feeding on top.

## What jighead to rig it on

- **1/4oz Sable Series Jighead Round** - the standard pairing for typical estuary flats
  depth (1-3m) with light to moderate current. This is the jighead we ship the Paddle Tail
  4" photographed on and the one to reach for first.
- Step up to **3/8oz** in stronger tidal current or water deeper than 3m so the plastic
  still reaches and holds bottom; drop to **1/8oz** in skinny water under 1m so the retrieve
  stays slow enough without snagging on the drop.

## Best water conditions

- **Clear water** - a natural swimming action and realistic colour reads best where
  flathead can sight the bait.
- **Current / moving tidal water** - cast up-current and let the paddle tail swim down and
  across on a slow retrieve, covering the sand edges where flathead sit and wait.

## How to work it

A **cast-and-wind steady retrieve** right along the bottom, slow enough that the jighead
occasionally ticks the sand. Flathead strikes are usually a solid thump, not a tap - hold
the rod tip down and let the fish load up before setting.

This manual sets out how a Publisher Mutual claims handler assesses and decides a general-insurance claim across the home, motor and commercial property lines. It is the standing reference for the claims desk and should be read with the relevant Product Disclosure Statement and the claimant's own policy schedule.

The claims workflow. Every claim moves through five stages: lodgement, triage, assessment, coverage determination, and decision or settlement. At lodgement the claimant submits a First Notice of Loss (FNOL) with the date of loss, a description, and any supporting evidence. At triage the handler confirms the policy is identified, records the peril, and routes the claim to the right assessor. At assessment the handler gathers the evidence needed to decide: the policy schedule, the relevant policy wording, any third-party reports (a plumber, a repairer, a police event reference, a loss adjuster or building inspector), and photographs. At coverage determination the handler applies the four-step test below. At decision or settlement the handler communicates a payable, not-payable or needs-more-information outcome, and where payable, settles by repair, replacement or cash.

The coverage-determination steps. A handler decides coverage by working through four questions in order, and must be able to point to the policy wording or schedule for each. First, is the peril a covered event under the relevant policy wording? Second, does any exclusion apply that removes cover for this loss, such as flood where flood cover was not selected, or gradual deterioration on a home water claim? Third, is the loss within the sums insured and any applicable sub-limit, such as the unspecified single-item contents sub-limit or a business-interruption sub-limit? Fourth, are the policy conditions met, including the duty of disclosure and, for motor, that the premium was not in arrears at the date of loss? A claim is payable only where the peril is covered, no exclusion applies, the loss is within limits, and the conditions are met.

Excess and sub-limits. The excess shown on the schedule is deducted from the amount otherwise payable for each claim before payment, unless an endorsement waives it, such as the Glass Excess Waiver on a windscreen claim. A sub-limit caps the amount payable for a particular category of loss regardless of the overall sum insured: an unspecified valuable is capped at the $2,500 single-item sub-limit unless it was listed as a specified item, and a business-interruption loss is capped at its own sub-limit even when the property sum insured is higher.

Settlement methods. A payable claim is settled by repair, by replacement, or by cash settlement, whichever is appropriate to the loss and the policy. Motor damage is usually settled by authorised repair; a total loss or theft is usually cash-settled at the vehicle's market value less the excess. Home and contents losses are settled by repair, replacement of the damaged item, or cash, less the excess and subject to any sub-limit.

Timeframes and updates, aligned to the General Insurance Code of Practice. Publisher Mutual acknowledges a claim within one business day, makes a decision within four months of receiving a claim, and gives the claimant an update at least every twenty business days while a claim is open. Where a claim needs more information before it can be decided, the handler records what is outstanding and requests it in writing.

Escalation. A handler escalates to a senior claims adjuster where the claim exceeds the handler's authority limit, where an integrity indicator has been flagged, or where the coverage position is genuinely arguable. A not-payable or partial decision on a home or motor claim above $10,000, and any commercial property claim, is reviewed by a senior claims adjuster before it is communicated.

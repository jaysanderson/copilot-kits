This reference lists the exception and integrity indicators a Publisher Mutual claims handler must actively check on every claim and flag when present. An indicator is not proof of anything by itself; it is a trigger that requires the handler to pause, gather more information, and where warranted refer the claim to investigations before it is settled. Flagging an indicator honestly protects both the insurer and the genuine claimant.

Date-of-loss inconsistency. Where the date of loss recorded on the First Notice of Loss does not match the date recorded on a third-party report - a police report for a theft, a repairer's invoice, a fire brigade report or a building inspector's note - the handler must flag a date-of-loss inconsistency. A material gap between the FNOL date and an independent report's date must be resolved before the claim is decided; the handler requests an explanation and documents it.

Premium in arrears or policy lapsed at the date of loss. The handler must confirm the premium payment status as at the date of loss. Where the premium was unpaid and the policy was in arrears, or the policy had lapsed, at the date of loss, cover may be reduced or refused to the extent the policy wording and the law allow. This check is mandatory on every theft and total-loss claim before settlement.

Claimed amount exceeding a sub-limit. Where the amount claimed for a category of loss exceeds the applicable sub-limit - an unspecified valuable above the $2,500 single-item contents sub-limit, or a business-interruption loss above its sub-limit - the handler must flag it and cap the payment at the sub-limit, explaining the cap to the claimant. A high-value item that should have been listed as a specified item but was not is the most common instance.

Duplicate or overlapping claim. Where the same loss appears to have been claimed more than once, or overlaps a claim on another policy, the handler must flag a possible duplicate and reconcile the claims before either is paid.

Prior-claims pattern. Where the claimant has a pattern of prior claims - several claims of the same peril in a short period, or a prior claim that was withdrawn before assessment completed - the handler must flag it for review. A pattern is context for assessment, not a reason to decline on its own.

Inconsistent damage cause. Where the physical evidence of how the damage occurred does not match the cause described on the FNOL - for example, damage that an inspector attributes to gradual deterioration when the claimant described a sudden event, or theft damage inconsistent with forced entry - the handler must flag an inconsistent damage cause and have the cause independently assessed before deciding coverage.

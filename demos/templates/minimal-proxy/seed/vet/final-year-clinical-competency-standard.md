# Final-Year Clinical Competency Standard: Day-One Skills and Sign-Off

> it is real. Do not cite it or act on it clinically.

## What the standard requires

A graduate must be able to perform a defined set of day-one skills safely and without direct supervision. The standard lists twenty-four skills. Every one must be signed off before the rotation coordinator can recommend a student for graduation. There is no pass mark across the list: twenty-three of twenty-four is a fail.

The twenty-four skills are grouped into five areas. Physical examination covers six skills, including a full examination of a dog, cat, horse and ruminant. Clinical procedures cover eight skills, including intravenous catheter placement, venepuncture in three species and urinary catheterisation. Anaesthesia and analgesia cover four skills, including induction, monitoring and recovery. Surgery covers four skills, including aseptic preparation, ovariohysterectomy and wound closure. Diagnostic and client skills cover two: radiograph positioning and a written discharge instruction.

## Three observed attempts

A skill is signed off as competent only after three directly observed attempts by a supervising clinician. The attempts need not be on the same day or in the same rotation. The supervisor must be in the room, watching, for the whole attempt. A supervisor who is in the building but not watching cannot count the attempt.

Each attempt is recorded in the student's competency log with the date, the species, the supervisor's name and one of three grades: Not Yet Competent, Competent With Prompting, or Competent. An attempt is a pass only if it is graded Competent. Sign-off needs three Competent attempts, and the last two must be consecutive. A Not Yet Competent grade after two Competent attempts resets the count to zero.

A student who is graded Not Yet Competent on the same skill three times in a row is referred to the rotation coordinator, who arranges a supervised practice session on a model or cadaver before the next live attempt.

## Who may sign

Only a registered veterinarian with at least two years of clinical practice may sign off a skill. Veterinary nurses, interns and residents in their first year can observe attempts and give feedback, but their observation does not count toward the three attempts. A supervisor signs the log entry within two working days of the attempt. An unsigned entry is not counted.

## Conflicts of interest

A student cannot sign off a skill on animals owned by their own family or friends, and cannot be signed off by a supervisor who is a relative. Attempts on a student's own animals are not logged. The student tells the rotation coordinator if a supervisor has a personal relationship with them, and the coordinator reassigns the supervision.

## Models, cadavers and live patients

Eleven of the twenty-four skills may be signed off on a model or cadaver for the first two attempts, but the third attempt must be on a live patient. The exceptions are ovariohysterectomy, intravenous catheter placement and anaesthetic induction, which need all three attempts on live patients under supervision. A cadaver attempt is graded using the same form.

## Tracking progress

The competency log is reviewed by the rotation coordinator at the end of each rotation. A student with fewer than six skills signed off at the end of the third rotation, or fewer than eighteen at the end of the sixth, is placed on a competency plan. The plan names the missing skills, the rotations where they will be practised and the supervisors who will observe.

## When a skill is not signed off

A skill not signed off by the end of the final rotation triggers a targeted competency block of up to ten working days. The block is built around the missing skills only. The student works with a named supervisor, and the supervisor can sign off a skill at the first Competent attempt in the block if the student already has two prior Competent attempts. A student who still has any unsigned skill at the end of the block is not recommended for graduation in that cycle and is enrolled in the next available block.

## Exemptions

No skill is waived on the grounds of a student's intended career. A student who plans to work only with small animals still needs the ruminant physical examination signed off. A student with a documented disability that prevents performing a skill in the usual way can request an adjusted method, which the head of the clinical teaching unit approves in writing before the first attempt.

## Records and appeals

Competency logs are kept for seven years after graduation. A student who disagrees with a Not Yet Competent grade can ask for a second observed attempt with a different supervisor within five working days. The second supervisor's grade replaces the first only if it is higher, and the first attempt remains in the log.

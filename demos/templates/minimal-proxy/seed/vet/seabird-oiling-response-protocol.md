## Scope

This protocol covers the first seven days of care for seabirds contaminated by fuel or crude oil. It applies to penguins, terns, gulls, cormorants and shearwaters admitted from the Western Australian coast. It does not cover plumage contaminated by non-petroleum substances, which is handled under a separate protocol.

## Arrival and triage

Staff wear gloves, eye protection and a disposable gown, and the birds are held in a ventilated space away from the main hospital. Each oiled bird is assigned a number, and the number is written on a leg band or a tag. The admitting veterinarian triages the bird into one of three groups within fifteen minutes.

Group A birds are bright, alert and able to stand. Group B birds are dull, weak or cold but have a realistic chance of recovery. Group C birds are unable to hold their head up, have severe injuries or have ingested oil in the lungs, shown by laboured breathing or an oil smell on the breath. Group C birds are euthanased on the day.

## Stabilisation before washing

An oiled seabird is stabilised for 24 to 48 hours before it is washed. A bird is not washed on arrival. Washing is stressful, and a bird that is cold, dehydrated or weak does not survive it.

Stabilisation covers warming, fluids and feeding. Birds are held in a room at 25 to 28 degrees Celsius. A cold bird with a body temperature below 38 degrees is warmed under a heat lamp before anything else. Dehydrated birds receive warmed subcutaneous or oral fluids at a rate of 50 millilitres per kilogram in the first twelve hours. Birds are tube-fed an electrolyte solution first, then a fish slurry once they have passed droppings.

Oil is absorbed by the bird if it preens. Birds are kept off water and given an absorbent surface, and eyes are flushed with saline to remove oil.

## Readiness to wash

A bird is washed only when it meets all of these criteria: a packed cell volume of at least 30 per cent, a total plasma protein of at least 35 grams per litre, a body temperature in the normal range for the species, alert behaviour and the ability to stand. A bird that is cold, dehydrated or has a low packed cell volume is not washed, and its blood is re-tested after twelve hours.

If a bird has not met the criteria after 72 hours, the veterinarian reassesses it for euthanasia.

## Washing

Washing uses a 1 per cent detergent solution at 40 degrees Celsius. The bird is washed in a series of three tubs by a team of two, and one person holds the bird while the other washes. The head is washed last, with a soft toothbrush, and the water is changed until it runs clear. A bird is washed for a maximum of 30 minutes per session. If the plumage is still contaminated after 30 minutes, the bird is dried and rested for 24 hours before a second wash.

Rinsing is done with fresh water at 40 degrees Celsius under low pressure until no detergent remains. Detergent left in the feathers destroys waterproofing, so rinsing takes at least as long as washing.

## Drying and waterproofing

After rinsing, the bird is dried in a warm room at 28 degrees Celsius, with a heat lamp, for at least two hours. The bird then preens itself. A bird that preens and rebuilds its feather structure regains waterproofing; one that does not is assessed for a second wash.

Waterproofing is tested in a pool. The bird goes into a pool of fresh water for 20 minutes at a time, under observation, and is taken out if it becomes wet to the skin. A bird is released only after it passes a 48-hour waterproofing test in a pool, which means it spends 48 hours with access to the pool and stays dry at the skin with water beading on the plumage. A bird that fails is dried and reassessed.

## Release criteria

A bird is released when it is waterproof, has maintained body weight for 72 hours, has a packed cell volume of at least 35 per cent, and shows normal behaviour. It is banded with a metal band from the state banding scheme before release. Release is to a clean site close to the point of origin, away from any remaining oil, and the release site and weather are recorded.

## Records and reporting

Every bird's triage group, bloods, wash dates and outcome are recorded. The numbers admitted, washed, released and euthanased are reported to the incident controller each day by 5 pm.

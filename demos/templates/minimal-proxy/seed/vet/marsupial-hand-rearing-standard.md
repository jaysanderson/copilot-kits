# Hand-Rearing Standard for Orphaned Marsupial Joeys in Western Australia

## Scope

This standard covers western grey kangaroo and brushtail possum joeys admitted without their mother. It sets out the minimum care a carer must provide, and the points at which a veterinarian must be involved.

## Assessing the joey on admission

A joey is first checked for injury, warmed and rehydrated before any milk is offered. A cold joey cannot digest, and feeding it causes bloat and aspiration. The joey is warmed to a body temperature of 35 degrees Celsius using a pouch lined with a wool liner and a heat source set at 30 degrees, never touching the joey.

Age is estimated from the stage of fur, ear and eye development and from pes length, the length of the hind foot. A joey with no fur and closed eyes is a pouch-young joey. A joey with fine fur and open eyes is an early furred joey. A joey fully furred, out of the pouch and eating grass is a late-stage joey. The carer records the pes length at admission and weekly, and the veterinarian confirms the age estimate within 48 hours.

## Milk

Joeys are fed a species-specific marsupial milk replacer and never cow's milk. Cow's milk contains too much lactose for a marsupial, which cannot digest it, and it causes diarrhoea, cataracts and death. Formula is mixed fresh for each feed, using boiled water cooled to 40 degrees Celsius, and any leftover is discarded after one hour. Teats are sterilised after each feed. Early-stage formula is used until the joey is furred, and a higher-fat formula is used from then until weaning.

## Feeding schedule

A pouch-young joey is fed every three hours at first, including overnight, and moves to four-hourly feeds once furred. A furred joey on four-hourly feeds moves to three feeds a day after its eyes have been open for four weeks. The volume per feed is 5 per cent of body weight in the first week and is raised only on the advice of the veterinarian.

The joey is held in a natural position, upright against the carer's body for a macropod and level for a possum, never on its back. After each feed the joey is stimulated to pass urine and faeces by gentle wiping of the cloaca with a warm, damp cloth until it can do so alone.

## Weight monitoring

Joeys are weighed every day, at the same time and on the same scale. The weight is plotted on a growth chart for the species. A loss of more than 5 per cent of body weight in 24 hours requires a veterinary check on the same day. A joey that gains nothing over three days is checked by the veterinarian as well.

## Weaning

A joey is weaned onto solids only after it is fully furred and its eyes are open. For kangaroo joeys, weaning begins with fresh grass, native browse and a pellet suited to macropods placed in the enclosure. The milk is reduced by one feed every week over six to eight weeks. A joey that stops gaining weight during weaning goes back one stage in the schedule.

Possum joeys are offered eucalyptus leaf, native flowers and a small amount of fruit from the age of eight weeks. Milk is withdrawn once the joey is eating independently and keeps its weight for seven days.

## Soft-release enclosure

A joey enters a soft-release enclosure at a minimum of 80 per cent of the species' expected release weight. The enclosure is at least 100 square metres for a kangaroo joey and contains shelter, natural vegetation and a second joey for company. Hand contact falls to one visit a day and no contact is made with people outside the carer team. The joey must not be trained to expect food from people.

A joey stays in the soft-release enclosure for at least four weeks. It is moved to release when it is feeding on its own, holds its weight for two weeks without milk and responds with alarm to unfamiliar people and dogs.

## Release

Release is to the point of origin, or within five kilometres of it where that site is unsafe, with the landowner's permission. Release is done at dusk in warm weather, with water and supplementary food left at the site for two weeks. Each release is recorded, with the site and the number of joeys released.

## Euthanasia criteria

A joey is euthanased on the veterinarian's advice if it has a developmental deformity, a spinal injury, a chronic cataract or an inability to feed after five days of treatment. A joey that cannot be released is not kept as a pet, and a carer may not keep a native animal without a permit.

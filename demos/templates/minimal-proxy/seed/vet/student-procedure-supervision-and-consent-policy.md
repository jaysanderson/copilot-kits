# Supervision and Client Consent for Student-Performed Procedures

## Three supervision levels

Every procedure a student may take part in is assigned one of three supervision levels. Level 1 procedures are low-risk and non-invasive, such as taking a temperature, listening to the chest and collecting a voided urine sample. A student may perform a Level 1 procedure with a supervisor in the building, available within five minutes, once the skill has been signed off.

Level 2 procedures carry moderate risk, such as venepuncture, intravenous catheter placement, subcutaneous injection and suturing skin. A student performs a Level 2 procedure only with a supervisor in the room, watching, who can take over at once. The student does not start a Level 2 procedure until the supervisor has said so.

Level 3 procedures carry high risk, such as induction of general anaesthesia, entering a body cavity, euthanasia and administering a controlled drug. A student may not perform a Level 3 procedure under any circumstances. A student may assist with a Level 3 procedure by handing instruments, monitoring and recording, with the supervisor performing the procedure.

A student who is unsure of the level asks the supervisor. If the procedure is not on the list, it is treated as Level 3 until the head of the clinical teaching unit assigns a level.

## Client consent

Written client consent is required before a student performs any invasive procedure on a client-owned animal. An invasive procedure is one that breaks the skin, enters a body cavity or requires sedation. Consent for Level 1 procedures is verbal and noted in the clinical record.

The consent form names the procedure, states that a student will perform it under the supervision of a named veterinarian, and states that the veterinarian remains responsible for the animal's care. The client is given the form, the time to read it and the chance to ask questions before signing. A client is told that refusing has no effect on the care the animal receives or on the fees charged.

A consent form signed more than 30 days before the procedure is not valid for a student-performed procedure. The client signs a new form, and the supervisor confirms with the client on the day that the consent still stands.

## When a client refuses

A client who refuses student involvement is not asked a second time and the animal receives the same standard of care. The supervisor performs the procedure or arranges for a qualified colleague to do it. The refusal is recorded in the clinical record, and the student is not told the reason. The student may still observe if the client agrees to observation.

A student never tries to persuade a client to consent, and never implies that care depends on it. A student who does so is reported to the rotation coordinator under the professional conduct domain.

## Emergencies

In an emergency, where the client cannot be reached and delay would cause suffering or death, a procedure may go ahead without a signed form. A supervisor performs any Level 2 or Level 3 procedure in this case. A student may assist only. The supervisor records the reason, the time and the attempt to reach the client, and the client is told as soon as they can be reached.

## Introducing the student

A student introduces themselves to the client by name and role at the start of every consultation, wears a name badge that states "Student" and does not use the title "Doctor". A client who asks a student for advice is told that the supervisor will answer. The student does not give a diagnosis, prognosis or cost estimate to a client unless the supervisor has approved it in advance.

## Supervisor responsibilities

A supervisor stays responsible for every animal in a student's care. The supervisor reviews and countersigns every clinical record a student writes before the end of the day. A supervisor does not supervise more than three students at once for Level 2 procedures, and only one at a time for the first attempt at a new skill.

A supervisor stops a student at once if the student shows signs of losing control, hesitating, or going outside the agreed procedure. The supervisor does not wait for harm to occur, and the stop is recorded in the rotation log.

## Teaching animals and shelter animals

Procedures on animals from the teaching colony and on shelter animals under a partnership agreement follow the same supervision levels. Consent is given by the colony manager or shelter manager in writing, and covers a list of named procedures, renewed every twelve months.

## Incidents and reporting

If a student-performed procedure causes harm, the supervisor treats the animal, tells the client the same day in person or by phone and records the incident. The incident is reported to the rotation coordinator within one working day. The student is not disciplined for an honest error made while following the policy. Failure to follow the policy is a conduct matter.

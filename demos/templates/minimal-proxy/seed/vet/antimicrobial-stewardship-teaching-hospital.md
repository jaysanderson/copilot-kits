# Antimicrobial Stewardship in the Teaching Hospital

## Why a teaching hospital prescribes differently

Every prescribing decision made in this hospital is watched by students who will carry the habit into practice for thirty years. The policy is written on that basis rather than on the basis of any single patient's convenience. A restricted drug given without scrutiny teaches forty students that scrutiny is optional.

## Which classes need senior approval

Third-generation cephalosporins (ceftriaxone and cefovecin are the two stocked) and fluoroquinolones (enrofloxacin and marbofloxacin) require senior clinician approval before they are dispensed. The senior clinician is a registrar or a specialist on the rostered service; a final-year student or a rotating intern cannot give approval, and neither can the prescriber approving their own order. Approval is recorded against the case in the clinical record, not given verbally, and the pharmacy will not release the drug without the recorded approval number.

Anti-pseudomonal penicillins, carbapenems, vancomycin and linezolid are not prescribed at all in this hospital. Where a case appears to need one, the patient is referred to a facility with an infectious disease service rather than treated here.

## Which agents are unrestricted

Any clinician may prescribe the following for any indication within label guidance: amoxicillin, amoxicillin with clavulanate, first-generation cephalosporins such as cefalexin, doxycycline, trimethoprim-sulfonamide combinations, metronidazole and clindamycin. Amoxicillin with clavulanate is the default first choice for skin, soft tissue and urinary tract infections in small animals when an antimicrobial is justified at all. Amoxicillin alone is the default for uncomplicated respiratory infection.

## How long an approval lasts

Approval for a restricted agent lasts 72 hours. To continue beyond 72 hours the prescriber must return to the approving clinician with a culture and sensitivity result attached. A restricted course that has no culture result at the 72 hour mark is stopped or changed to an unrestricted agent. The approving clinician records one of three outcomes: continue, narrow to a named unrestricted agent, or stop.

## Culture and sensitivity

Culture and sensitivity is required before any restricted-class course continues beyond forty-eight hours. An empirical start is acceptable; an empirical continuation is not. The sample for culture is collected before the first dose wherever the patient's condition allows. If the first dose has already been given, the sample is still collected and the report is annotated with the time of the dose. Urine is collected by cystocentesis, not from the floor or a free-catch, when culture will drive a restricted-class decision.

## Duration limits

Default course lengths are written into the formulary and the prescriber must justify exceeding them. Uncomplicated urinary tract infection is three to five days. Superficial pyoderma is seven to fourteen days and is extended only after re-examination. Perioperative prophylaxis is a single dose at induction, repeated only if the procedure exceeds ninety minutes, and never continued after the patient has recovered from anaesthesia. Post-operative antimicrobials are not given to clean elective procedures.

## Surgical prophylaxis

Clean surgery receives no antimicrobial beyond the induction dose, and often not that. Cefazolin at induction is the hospital standard for orthopaedic implants. A clean-contaminated procedure, such as a gastrointestinal resection, receives induction dosing plus a repeat at ninety minutes. A dirty procedure is treated as an infection and is cultured intra-operatively.

## Audit

Prescribing is audited monthly. The audit is taught rather than filed: the month's restricted-class approvals are reviewed in the clinical rounds, with the approving clinician present. The audit counts three things: the proportion of restricted-class courses with a culture result by 72 hours, the proportion with a recorded duration, and the number of approvals that were later narrowed. The hospital target is a culture result on at least 90 per cent of restricted-class courses beyond 72 hours. The purpose is not compliance, and a clinician whose approvals are consistently sound has nothing to defend.

## What students are expected to do

A student who believes a restricted drug is needed brings the case, the suspected organism, the sample already collected and the unrestricted alternative they considered and rejected. A student who cannot name the alternative has not finished thinking about the case, and the approving clinician will send them back to do so.

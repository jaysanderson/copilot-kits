# Assessing a Student on Clinical Rotation: What Is Actually Being Marked

## What is being marked

Clinical rotation assessment is not an examination of knowledge, which has already been examined. It assesses whether a student can act safely and usefully in a working hospital. Each rotation runs for three weeks, and the clinical supervisor completes a written assessment of every student at the end of week one, week two and week three. The week-one and week-two assessments are formative and do not count toward the result. The week-three assessment is the summative mark.

## Four domains

Clinical reasoning, practical competence, communication and professional conduct are marked separately. A strong performance in one does not compensate for a weak one in another, and the final mark is not an average. Each domain is graded on a four-point scale: Unsafe, Developing, Meets Expectations and Exceeds Expectations. To pass a rotation a student must reach Meets Expectations in all four domains. A student who reaches Exceeds Expectations in three domains and Developing in the fourth does not pass.

A student graded Developing in one domain at the end of week three is given a single supplementary period of five working days in the same discipline. The rotation coordinator sets the learning goals for that period in writing within two working days of the final assessment. A student graded Developing in two or more domains repeats the whole rotation.

## The domain students misunderstand

Recognising the limit of one's own competence is assessed separately from clinical knowledge, and it is the domain students most often misread. It sits inside the professional conduct domain. A student who asks for help at the right moment is marked up, not down. The right moment is before starting a procedure the student has not performed under direct supervision, before giving a drug dose the student has not checked with a second person, and before telling a client something the student is not certain is correct.

A student who proceeds past their competence and is rescued by a supervisor is marked down even when the outcome is fine, because the outcome was the supervisor's. The supervisor records the event in the rotation log with the date, the task and what the student did before stopping or failing to stop.

Asking for help too often is not penalised in the first rotation of the final year. From the third rotation onward, a student who asks for help on tasks already signed off as competent is marked down in clinical reasoning, because the question shows the student has not retained the earlier teaching.

## Unsafe acts

A single unsafe act is not averaged against good performance elsewhere. It is recorded, discussed on the day, and assessed on its own. This is the one place where the rubric departs from proportionality, and it does so deliberately.

Unsafe acts include administering a drug without a second-person dose check, handling a fractious animal without the restraint the supervisor specified, discharging a patient or giving a client a result without supervisor approval, and falsifying or back-filling a clinical record. The supervisor grades the relevant domain Unsafe, and the rotation coordinator is told the same day. A student with one Unsafe grade does not pass that rotation. The student repeats it in full, and the second attempt is supervised by a different clinician.

Falsifying a record is also referred to the professional conduct committee, whatever the clinical outcome.

## Missed time and attendance

A student must attend every day of a rotation. A student who misses up to two days in a rotation week with a documented reason, such as illness with a medical certificate, makes up the time by arrangement with the rotation coordinator before the end of the following rotation. A student who misses more than two days in a rotation week has not met the minimum clinical exposure for that week, and the rotation coordinator decides whether the whole week or the whole rotation is repeated. The decision and the reason are recorded in the rotation log.

An absence without notice is a professional conduct matter. The student is graded on conduct for that week regardless of the reason once it is explained.

## Feedback timing

Feedback is given on the day rather than at the end of the rotation. A student who learns in week four what they were doing wrong in week one has lost three weeks. The supervisor gives each student a short verbal debrief of at least five minutes at the end of each clinical day, and notes in the rotation log the single most important thing said.

A student who disagrees with a grade can ask for a review within five working days of receiving it. The review is done by a second supervisor who has not worked with the student, using the rotation log and the written assessments only. The second supervisor's grade replaces the first.

## Evidence the supervisor uses

The supervisor grades from direct observation, the rotation log, case records the student wrote, and the views of nurses and technicians who worked with the student. Client comments are used only to inform the communication domain and never lower a grade by themselves. A grade is not given on a single observation unless it records an unsafe act.

## Who decides what

The clinical supervisor assigns the domain grades. The rotation coordinator confirms the result, schedules any supplementary period or repeat, and keeps the rotation log. The head of the clinical teaching unit hears review requests that the second supervisor cannot resolve.

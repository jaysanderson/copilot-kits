# Histopathology Turnaround and Specimen Fixation

## How long a result takes

Routine histopathology is reported within 5 working days of receipt, and urgent cases within 2 working days. The clock starts when the specimen is booked in at the laboratory, not when it was collected. An urgent report is requested on the form and a telephone call is made, and a specimen not fixed properly cannot be made urgent. Large specimens such as an amputated limb, or specimens with multiple margins, take 7 to 10 working days. Decalcification of bone adds 3 to 7 days to the turnaround, and the report is delayed accordingly. Special stains, such as periodic acid-Schiff for fungi or Ziehl-Neelsen for acid-fast bacteria, add 1 day. Immunohistochemistry adds 3 to 5 days.

## The fixative

Tissue is fixed in 10 per cent neutral buffered formalin at a ratio of at least 10 parts of formalin to 1 of tissue. Less than that exhausts the fixative and the centre of the specimen autolyses. Fixation takes 24 to 48 hours before processing. Formalin penetrates about 1 centimetre in 24 hours, which is why specimens thicker than 1 centimetre are sliced before fixation. Slices are cut with a sharp blade, not scissors, to avoid crush, and one face is left connected to keep orientation.

## Special tissues

Brain is fixed whole for a minimum of 5 to 7 days before cutting, or is cut in half sagittally and half submitted fresh. Lung is inflated gently with formalin through the bronchus, or sliced and submerged weighted with a gauze cover so it does not float. Intestine is opened along its length, rinsed gently and laid flat on card, or small rings of 1 centimetre are fixed. Eyes are injected with a small volume of formalin through the sclera, then fixed whole. Skin biopsies are placed on a card, epidermis up, before immersion to prevent curling. Bladder is lightly distended with formalin.

## Biopsy size and number

Punch biopsies of 6 millimetres are preferred for skin and take 3 from different lesions at different stages. An excision for a mass is submitted whole, with the margins inked, not trimmed. For liver, 3 separate samples of 1 centimetre are taken from different lobes. A needle core biopsy of 14 gauge is accepted for liver, kidney and masses, but 16 gauge is too small for most diagnoses.

## Do not freeze, do not mix

Frozen tissue is not suitable for routine histopathology because ice crystals disrupt the cells. Fresh tissue for culture or PCR goes in a separate pot. Do not put formalin-fixed and fresh tissue in the same container. Formalin that has turned yellow or has become acidic is replaced. Formalin is a hazard: it is a carcinogen, and a spilt litre is cleaned with the spill kit with ventilation.

## Posting

Fixed tissue is posted in a leakproof pot inside a sealed plastic bag with absorbent material. For postage, once fixed for 24 to 48 hours, the excess formalin is poured off and the tissue wrapped in formalin-soaked gauze inside a bag, which satisfies postal dangerous goods limits. Specimens are not posted on a Friday, since the weekend delay causes drying.

## Reasons for an unsuitable specimen

A specimen is reported as unsuitable, or as limited, when it was frozen, when it was autolysed before fixation, when it was dried, when it was too small to be representative, or when crush artefact obscured the architecture. In the last audit, 9 per cent of specimens had limiting artefact, and the leading cause was delay between death and fixation of more than 6 hours.

## What the form must say

The form states the species, age, sex, site, the lesion's appearance and duration, and the clinical question. It says whether margins are to be assessed. It gives a contact telephone number. A request with no site recorded is held for 24 hours while the laboratory phones the submitter.

## Reports

The report has a gross description, a microscopic description, a morphological diagnosis and a comment. The morphological diagnosis is the one-line answer, and it is given first. Where the diagnosis is uncertain the report lists the differentials in order of likelihood.

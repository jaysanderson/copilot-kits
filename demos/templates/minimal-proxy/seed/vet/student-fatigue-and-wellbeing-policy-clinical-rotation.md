# Student Fatigue and Wellbeing Policy for Clinical Rotation

## Why the policy exists

A tired student makes dosing errors, misses details and is more likely to be injured by an animal. The policy sets fixed limits so that fatigue is managed by the roster and not left to the student's willingness to speak up.

## Hours

A student on rotation works no more than ten hours in a clinical day and fifty hours in a week. The ten hours include handovers and record-keeping. A student who is asked to stay beyond ten hours to finish a procedure tells the supervisor and leaves unless the supervisor judges that stopping would harm the patient. Where a student stays, the time is recorded and the excess is taken as time off in the next three days.

A student must have at least eleven hours between the end of one shift and the start of the next. A roster that breaks this limit is invalid, and the rotation coordinator is responsible for correcting it.

## Overnight and on-call duty

A student may be rostered for overnight on-call duty in the emergency and critical care rotation only, and for no more than two nights in any seven-day period. A student on overnight duty is rostered to sleep in the student room, and is called only for admissions and patients needing active monitoring. A student who has worked an overnight on-call shift is not rostered for the following day. A student who is called out between midnight and 5 am for more than two hours in total has the day after as rest even if their roster shows no on-call duty.

Students are not rostered for on-call duty in the first week of any rotation, because the first week is used to learn the hospital's systems.

## Breaks

A student has a thirty-minute meal break within five hours of starting and a ten-minute break in each half of the shift. A break is taken away from the clinical floor. A supervisor who cannot release a student for a break arranges cover and does not ask the student to wait.

## Stopping for fatigue

A student can stop work for fatigue without penalty by telling the supervisor. The supervisor must record the stop in the rotation log, with the time and the task the student was doing, but must not grade it or comment on it in the assessment. A student who stops for fatigue is not counted as absent. The student can return after rest, and if they leave for the rest of the day, the supervisor reports it to the rotation coordinator.

A supervisor who sees signs of fatigue, such as repeated errors, falling asleep or unusual irritability, can send the student to rest. The student is not penalised for this either.

If the same student stops for fatigue three times in a rotation, the rotation coordinator meets the student within three working days. The aim is to find out whether the roster, outside work or health is the cause, and not to discipline.

## Illness

A student who is unwell with an infectious illness stays away until symptom-free for 24 hours. A student with vomiting or diarrhoea stays away for 48 hours after the last episode. A student with a fever above 38 degrees stays away until the fever has gone without medicines for 24 hours. The student tells the rotation coordinator before the start of the shift, by message or phone, and does not need to explain the illness.

Time missed for illness is handled under the attendance rules in the rotation assessment rubric.

## Wellbeing support

Every student is allocated a wellbeing contact who is not involved in assessing them. The contact meets the student for thirty minutes at the end of the first week of each rotation. A student can also see the university counselling service, which offers appointments within three working days and a same-day phone call for urgent concerns.

After a euthanasia that a student found distressing, or after the death of a patient the student was caring for, the supervisor offers a ten-minute debrief that day and the student can take the rest of the day off without it counting against their time. Students are not required to attend more than two euthanasias in a single day.

## Pregnancy and health adjustments

A student who is pregnant or has a health condition can request adjusted duties from the rotation coordinator. Tasks involving anaesthetic gas, radiation, certain drugs and zoonotic risk are reassigned within five working days of the request. The adjustment is confidential and recorded only by the coordinator.

## Reporting breaches

A student who believes a roster breaks this policy reports it to the rotation coordinator, or to the head of the clinical teaching unit if the coordinator is the source of the problem. A report made in good faith is never held against the student.

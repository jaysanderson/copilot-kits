## How chronic kidney disease is staged

Staging is done once the animal is stable and well hydrated. Staging uses fasted creatinine and SDMA on a stable patient, then substages by proteinuria and blood pressure. Two readings at least 2 weeks apart are used, because a single value in a dehydrated or recently ill animal overstates the stage. The stage guides treatment, and the substage identifies the extra risk that needs separate treatment.

## Stage thresholds

For cats, stage 1 has creatinine below 140 micromoles per litre with an abnormal feature such as persistent proteinuria or small kidneys. Stage 2 is 140 to 250, stage 3 is 251 to 440, and stage 4 is above 440. For dogs, stage 1 has creatinine below 125 micromoles per litre, stage 2 is 125 to 250, stage 3 is 251 to 440, and stage 4 is above 440. SDMA is persistently raised above 14 micrograms per decilitre in stage 2, and may identify early disease earlier than creatinine, especially in cats and lean dogs. Urine specific gravity below 1.035 in dogs and 1.040 in cats suggests loss of concentrating ability.

## Substaging

Proteinuria is measured by the urine protein to creatinine ratio on a cystocentesis sample, with a result below 0.2 being non-proteinuric, 0.2 to 0.5 borderline and above 0.5 proteinuric in dogs, with cat thresholds of 0.2 and 0.4. Blood pressure is measured with a Doppler or high-definition oscillometric device, in a calm room after 10 minutes of acclimation, as the mean of 5 readings. Risk is classed as minimal under 140, low at 140 to 159, moderate at 160 to 179 and severe at 180 and above.

## Diet

A renal diet restricted in phosphate and protein is started at stage 2 in cats and at stage 3 in dogs, or earlier if the urine protein to creatinine ratio is above 0.5. Renal diets reduce uraemic crises and prolong survival. Transition is done over 7 to 10 days, and for cats that refuse it, a different texture, flavour or brand is tried, and warming the food helps. A cat that will not eat a renal diet is better fed its preferred food than left to starve, as maintaining calories matters more than restricting protein at any stage. Omega-3 supplementation with fish oil is recommended, and the diet should be wet where possible.

## Phosphate control

Target serum phosphate is below 1.5 millimoles per litre at stage 2, below 1.6 at stage 3 and below 1.9 at stage 4. Phosphate is measured on a fasted sample every 3 months once started. If diet alone does not achieve the target after 4 to 8 weeks, an intestinal phosphate binder such as aluminium hydroxide or lanthanum carbonate is mixed with food, starting at 30 milligrams per kilogram per day divided between meals, and adjusted by serum phosphate.

## Blood pressure

Amlodipine is started in cats when systolic blood pressure is persistently above 160 millimetres of mercury on repeated measurements, at 0.625 milligrams per cat once daily, and is rechecked in 7 to 14 days. In dogs, an ACE inhibitor or telmisartan is used first when hypertension is accompanied by proteinuria. Blood pressure is rechecked every 3 months in treated animals. Retinal examination is performed in hypertensive animals because retinal detachment may cause sudden blindness.

## Proteinuria

Dogs and cats with a persistent urine protein to creatinine ratio above 0.5 in dogs, or above 0.4 in cats, receive an ACE inhibitor or telmisartan with the aim of halving the ratio. Renal function, potassium and blood pressure are checked 7 to 10 days after starting treatment. Treatment is withheld in dehydrated animals.

## Hydration, anaemia and nausea

Fresh water is always available, and fluids are given subcutaneously at 20 to 50 millilitres per kilogram every 24 to 72 hours to stage 3 and 4 patients with dehydration. Anaemia from stage 3 is treated with an erythropoiesis stimulating agent when the packed cell volume is below 20 per cent in cats and 25 per cent in dogs. Maropitant, an antacid and an appetite stimulant such as mirtazapine are used for nausea and anorexia.

## Monitoring schedule

Stage 1 and 2 patients are reviewed every 3 to 6 months. Stage 3 and 4 patients are reviewed every 6 to 8 weeks. Each review records body weight, body condition and muscle condition scores, blood pressure, creatinine, SDMA, phosphate, potassium, packed cell volume and urine protein to creatinine ratio.

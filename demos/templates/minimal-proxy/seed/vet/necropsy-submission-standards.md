# Necropsy Submission Standards for Production Animal Cases

## Why standards rather than guidance

A post-mortem submission is a single-use sample. Where it arrives in a state that cannot be interpreted, nothing can be repeated, because the animal is dead and the window has closed. These are therefore standards: a submission that does not meet them is accepted, but the report states which questions it could not answer.

## How fast the carcase must be examined

A whole carcase should be examined within 12 hours of death if it has been chilled, and within 4 hours if it has not. In a WA summer, with ambient temperatures above 30 degrees, autolysis of the gut and liver is visible within 2 to 3 hours and intestinal histology is lost soon after. A carcase that has been dead more than 24 hours unchilled is not worth transporting for gross examination of the gut, though bone, hoof and some brain and lung findings may survive.

Carcases are chilled to 4 degrees, never frozen. Freezing ruptures cells and makes histology unreadable. A carcase that has been frozen is accepted only for toxicology and for gross skeletal findings.

## What is submitted fresh and what is fixed

Fresh tissue, chilled and not fixed, is submitted for microbiology, PCR, toxicology and virology. Fixed tissue is submitted for histopathology. The two are never in the same bag. Fixed and fresh tissue go in separate containers, because formalin vapour destroys the organisms that culture is meant to find and fluid from fresh tissue dilutes the fixative.

Fixed tissue goes into 10 per cent neutral buffered formalin at ten times the tissue's own volume. A specimen thicker than one centimetre is sliced before fixation, or the centre will not fix. Formalin penetrates about one centimetre in 24 hours, so a whole organ left uncut autolyses in the middle while the surface fixes. Hollow organs are opened or lightly distended with formalin. Lung is floated or the cut surface fixed whole in a larger container, because inflated lung floats out of the fluid.

## Which tissues, for which suspected disease

For sudden death in sheep and cattle, submit fresh small intestine content and a fixed set of liver, kidney, lung, heart, spleen and brain. For suspected enterotoxaemia, intestinal content is collected within an hour of death or the toxin degrades. For neurological disease, half the brain is fixed whole and the other half is submitted fresh and chilled. The head is not cut off and posted without arrangement; contact the laboratory first. For suspected abortion, submit the fetus, the placenta, and a clotted maternal blood sample taken at the time and again 14 to 21 days later.

## Biosecurity and packaging

Anthrax is excluded before any opening of a carcase of sudden death in cattle or sheep in a known risk area: a blood smear from an ear vein or a swab of exuded blood is examined first, and the carcase is not opened until the result is back. Leptospira, Salmonella and Q fever organisms are handled at containment level two. Samples travel in double sealed containers with absorbent material between, in a rigid outer box, and are not posted on Fridays.

## History and differential list

Every submission carries a history and a differential list. The history states species, breed, age, number affected out of number at risk, days of illness, treatments given and when, feed and paddock changes, vaccination status and the date and time of death. The differential list states the three diseases the submitting vet most suspects, so the pathologist can choose which tissues to examine first. A submission without a history and a differential list is frequently unanswerable, and the laboratory phones the submitter before starting rather than after.

## Autolysis is the usual failure

Autolysis, not disease, is the most common reason a submission cannot be interpreted. In the audit year, 31 per cent of production animal submissions were reported with autolysis limiting the diagnosis. Most arrived more than 24 hours after death, unchilled. A diagnosis was reached in 68 per cent of submissions chilled within 4 hours of death, and in 29 per cent of those that were not.

## Turnaround

Gross findings are reported by phone within one working day. Histopathology on correctly fixed tissue is reported within five working days. Culture takes two to three days for a negative and up to seven for a slow-growing organism. The final written report follows when the last result is complete.

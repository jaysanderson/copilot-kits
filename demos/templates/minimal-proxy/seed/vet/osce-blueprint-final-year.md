# Final-Year OSCE Blueprint: Stations, Timing and Marking

## Structure of the examination

The final-year objective structured clinical examination has twelve stations. Each station runs for eight minutes, followed by a two-minute changeover, so a full circuit takes two hours. Students rotate through the circuit together, and every student sits the same twelve stations in a different order. A student has two minutes before each station begins to read the instruction on the door.

Four stations are communication stations, using trained role players who act as clients. Four are practical procedure stations, using models or cadaver material. Two are interpretation stations, in which the student reads a radiograph, a blood result or a cytology image and states a plan. Two are clinical reasoning stations, in which a case summary is given and the student explains a differential list and next steps to an examiner.

## Examiners

Each station is marked by one examiner. An examiner is a registered veterinarian who has completed the examiner briefing within the last twelve months. Examiners do not mark stations in their own discipline for students they have supervised in the last six months. A second examiner moderates one station in every circuit, selected at random, and the two marks are compared at the end of the day.

## Marking

Each station is marked against a checklist of between eight and twelve items, plus a global rating on a five-point scale from Clear Fail to Excellent. Checklist items are marked done or not done, with no partial credit. The checklist produces a percentage, and the global rating is recorded separately.

A station is passed when the checklist score is at least 60 per cent and the global rating is Borderline Pass or above. A student can score 80 per cent on the checklist and still fail the station if the examiner gives a global rating of Fail. The examiner writes one sentence explaining any global rating of Fail or Clear Fail.

## Critical safety errors

A station fails automatically if the student commits a critical safety error, whatever the checklist score. Critical safety errors are defined for each station before the examination and printed on the examiner sheet. Examples are giving a drug dose ten times the correct amount, contaminating a sterile field and continuing, and disclosing a client's information to a person who is not the owner. An examiner who records a critical safety error stops the station and notes the time.

## Pass rule

A student must pass at least ten of the twelve stations and both communication stations to pass the OSCE. A student who passes ten stations but fails a communication station has failed the OSCE. A student who passes eleven stations including all four communication stations has passed.

The OSCE is a hurdle, not a graded assessment. The result is Pass or Fail, and the percentage scores are used only for feedback.

## Resit

A student who fails the OSCE sits a resit of six stations within six weeks. The six stations are those the student failed, topped up with stations from the same domain if the student failed fewer than six. The pass rule for the resit is five of six stations, including any communication station that was failed at first sitting. A student who fails the resit meets the head of the clinical teaching unit, and a remediation plan is written before a second resit is offered. A student is allowed a maximum of two resits.

## Standard setting

The pass mark for each station is set before the examination using a modified Angoff process, in which a panel of five clinicians estimates the proportion of borderline students who would complete each checklist item. The panel agrees a station cut score, and a station's cut score can vary between 55 and 70 per cent. The 60 per cent figure above is the default used when no panel has reviewed a station.

## Reasonable adjustments

A student with an approved adjustment, such as extra reading time or a quiet room, is told the arrangements at least ten working days before the examination. Adjustments do not change the checklist or the pass rule.

## After the examination

Results are released to students within ten working days. Each student receives the checklist for every failed station, with the items missed, and a short summary of examiner comments. Students do not see the checklists for stations they passed.
